# Product

## Register

product

## Users

JobVault serves three primary user groups working through practical hiring tasks. Job seekers upload resumes, review ranked job matches, inspect missing skills, and manage applications without re-entering the same profile data repeatedly. Employers create job postings, review candidate matches, and assess skill fit quickly enough to prioritize outreach. Admins monitor parsing and matching health, manage users and roles, and moderate postings so the platform remains reliable and compliant.

## Product Purpose

JobVault is a focused job portal that turns resume and job-description text into useful, deterministic hiring guidance. Its purpose is to reduce manual effort for seekers, improve candidate discovery for employers, and give admins clear operational visibility into matching, parsing, moderation, and authorization flows. Success means users can complete core hiring workflows with confidence, understand why matches or skill gaps appear, and recover cleanly when parsing, matching, or authentication fails.

## Brand Personality

Calm, focused, human. The interface should feel purposeful and steady, with subtle feedback instead of celebration. It should be minimal with warmth: enough breathing room to lower cognitive load, enough detail to make states and next actions clear, and enough restraint that users stay oriented during high-stakes career and hiring decisions.

## Anti-references

Avoid generic job-board blue, startup-gradient SaaS visuals, playful gamified career-app patterns, and corporate HR portal heaviness. Do not make the product feel noisy, celebratory, sales-led, or bureaucratic. Avoid decorative dashboards, gratuitous animation, over-branded empty states, and vague AI-style match explanations that reduce trust.

## Design Principles

1. Keep the task in front: every screen should make the current workflow, next action, and system state obvious.
2. Make matching explainable: scores, factors, skill gaps, and failure codes should be legible enough for users to trust the result.
3. Use warmth with restraint: the UI should feel human through spacing, language, and subtle details, not through decoration.
4. Prefer quiet confidence: feedback should be clear and timely without celebration, drama, or unnecessary motion.
5. Respect operational reality: admin and error states are first-class product surfaces, not afterthoughts.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

Target WCAG 2.2 AA for contrast, keyboard access, focus visibility, form labeling, and status messaging. Support reduced motion for nonessential transitions. Avoid relying on color alone for status, match quality, error severity, or required action. Keep language plain, especially around authentication, parsing failures, match factors, and skill gaps.
